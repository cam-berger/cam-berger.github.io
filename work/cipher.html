02 · HUMAN–AI-INTERACTION

CIPHER

Decision support for Army operational commanders. Turning dense textual and graphical information into a trusted knowledge graph for agentic reasoning.

Years
2025–2026 · Draper Laboratory
My role
Author. Research on representation and on augmenting human awareness
Publication
To appear at I/ITSEC 2026

The problem

Leaders are handed new orders and have to turn pages of dense, doctrine-shaped text into a plan, on the front line and on a clock. The manual processing removes the commander’s ability to think strategically and safely.

What I built

Since the paper, a harness of MCP tools to serve CIPHER. Given a set of order documents, it extracts and builds an interpretable knowledge graph that can be used for a multitude of purposes, such as an operations map, a timeline, and a plan flowchart; it verifies its own output against hallucinations and leverages reasoning to propose how the plan could be carried out. The leader works from an interactive generative interface instead of just a chat.

Method

  1. 1

    Represent

    Orders and plans are normalized into an adaptable schema, then rendered as a map, a timeline, and a flow.

  2. 2

    Judge

    A language model scores each output against the knowledge graph, source documents, and doctrine and flags what it cannot trace.

  3. 3

    Refine

    An automated pipeline evaluates and improves the outputs and toolkit to improve alignment with subject matter experts.

What I learned

Using a language model as the judge worked once prompts were refined, but it has its own limitations and drawbacks. Safe recommendations need to be derived directly from source information, and multimodal interfaces with human-in-the-loop review are necessary for foundational understanding.

What a bad recommendation costs

A bad recommendation costs trust.

That is the safety problem. AI in these settings has to sit inside a bounded system where risk assessment is real, and every output has to be traceable and honest.

Where it goes next

Changing the abstraction and representation of the event, or of the order itself, to give CIPHER and commanders true situational awareness.

This page contains

The high-level idea, what the I/ITSEC paper makes public, and where the work has actively gone.

This page does not contain

Draper proprietary design, code, or data, and no operational material.

Why it matters

This is the human-computer interaction half of my work: keeping a model’s recommendations traceable and honest while also aiding their situational awareness for action. It is part of the human-facing layer that my objectives outline.
