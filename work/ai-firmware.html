AI-Firmware

Give an agent the firmware from a device it has never seen. It works out what the device does and writes a standard interface any AI or person can use.

Years
2026 · Draper Laboratory
My role
Lead investigator and engineer
Written for
All three groups, Responsive Environments first
Status
Early positive results. Described at a high level pending release.

The problem

Most hardware runs hand-written firmware with no standard interface. Only the engineers who wrote it can read it, and the interfaces they shipped are usually poor. Every time a system needs a new sensor, a person has to integrate it by hand.

What I built

A toolkit and workflow that pulls the flash from a microcontroller and hands the binary to an agent. The agent probes and interprets the code from the outside, writes a standard interface for the device, and runs its own experiments on the hardware to confirm what each function does. It runs offline, at the edge.

At Draper I am pointing it at the bio lab: the sensors and equipment that run PREDICT96, Draper’s high-throughput organ-on-chip platform for testing drugs on human tissue, and the experiments we can now run through the drivers it writes.

How it works

  1. 1

    Read

    Pull the flash image from the device and give the agent the binary, with no source and no documentation.

  2. 2

    Probe

    The agent interprets the code, forms a hypothesis about each function, and tests it on the live hardware.

  3. 3

    Standardize

    It writes an interface that any AI or person can read and use for downstream tasks.

Result

On three emulated microcontrollers it had never seen, the agent finished two end to end with no human input. Both drivers passed every final check, and a fresh agent used each one to reach its goal.

Early results are positive on microcontrollers without encrypted firmware. In the latest benchmark run, one attempt per device, the two drivers passed 9 of 9 and 85 of 85 final checks in about ten minutes each; the third device, a parameter store, stopped during probing. These numbers come from the public benchmark’s emulated devices.

What’s next

Encrypted firmware is out of reach for now. The next step is the one I want to take at the Media Lab: an agent that walks into an environment, learns every device in it this way, and turns the whole space into something a person can understand and act in. CRESt, from Ju Li’s lab at MIT, showed what an AI can do with lab equipment that people integrated for it. I want to know whether an AI can integrate the equipment itself.

Why this belongs in Responsive Environments

The group builds distributed sensor networks, and someone always has to write the drivers. This is a way for a network to assemble itself. HexSense’s swappable payloads are a natural first test: an agent that learns each new payload on its own would let a node accept a sensor nobody wrote a driver for.

Tools

Python, with Java for Ghidra scripts. Agents run in the OpenAI Codex CLI and reach the toolkit over MCP. Ghidra (headless decompilation), Renode (emulated STM32 devices), the Arm GNU toolchain, and esptool. pyserial, pyusb, and pyftdi over UART, I²C, SPI, USB, and TCP. Hardware support: ESP32 flash reads, FTDI FT232H adapters, and a Bosch BME280 sensor.

Disclosure

The general tool is public as Generative Driver (Apache-2.0). Its Draper use is described at a high level only, pending release: no Draper code, data, or device specifics.
