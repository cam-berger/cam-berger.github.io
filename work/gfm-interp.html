03 · Interpretability

GFM-Interp

What has a scientific foundation model actually learned? In a masked genome model, I traced how it learned splice-site computation.

Years
2026 · Summer AI Safety Research Cohort
My role
Lead author, researcher, and engineer
Links

The problem

Scientific foundation models make accurate predictions, but there is limited research on how they learn complex genomic signals. When a genome model finds a splice site, is it using biology, or simply sequence statistics? The answer decides whether we can trust it on sequences it has never seen.

What I built

A measurement and a toolkit. I defined a measurement for the model’s splice-site decision, then adapted interpretability methods built for language models to a genome model, dissecting the nodes and edges that carry the decision.

How it works

  1. 1

    Measure

    Define a logit-difference measurement for the model’s splice-site decision.

  2. 2

    Attribute

    Rank the attention heads and paths that carry it, and decompose them with QK/OV analysis.

  3. 3

    Decompose

    Fourier-decompose the positional signal, which exposes a period-three component.

Result

The model reconstructs splice sites from boundary content and a learned three-nucleotide positional wave, with no splice-specific training.

On 400 sealed test sequences, corrupting the paired acceptor changes the masked-donor margin by 0.872 logit units (95% CI 0.771–0.969); matched controls give 0.007 and 0.005. Ablating the two heads the scan found, L3H1 and L2H15, removes 81.6% of that coupling, more than any of 400 random head pairs. Nested activation-patching interventions assign four causal roles: coding sequence positional geometry supplies an address, early heads and MLPs build an acceptor-side payload, L3H1 writes it to the donor, and later heads deliver it. Deleting the learned period-3 component cuts coupling from 0.946 to 0.026 logit units. The core is real but not self-sufficient: 90% faithfulness takes 64 of the 103 measured nodes.

What broke, what’s next

Some of what the model learned is not robust. Simple perturbations remove its confidence, and some well-known biology is only weakly encoded in training. That looks like shortcut learning rather than a robust representation, and it suggests that a better representation of genomic training data and architectures would be more robust. The period-three component is where I would start building that representation theory.

Why it matters

A genomic sequence is a modality with no words in it. This work is an instrument for looking inside how a model learns and, thus, into how transformers represent structure.

Tools
