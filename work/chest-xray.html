Multimodal chest X-ray

Can clinical context make an image classifier better at finding anomalies? A fine-tuned vision model fused with standardized patient records.

  • Multimodal ML
Years
2025–2026 · Independent research
My role
Sole researcher and engineer
Written for
Multisensory Intelligence
Status
First iteration complete, January 2026.

The problem

A radiologist never reads a chest X-ray alone. They know why the patient came in, their vital signs, and their labs, and that context changes what they look for. Most models see only the image, and labeling images well enough to train them is slow and expensive.

What I built

A pipeline that links each X-ray in MIMIC-CXR to the emergency-department visit around it, and a classifier that reads the image together with that visit. Its image encoder first learns what a normal chest looks like, from normal X-rays alone, before it ever sees a label.

How it works

  1. 1

    Link

    Match each X-ray to an emergency visit within 24 hours, and split the studies into about 20,000 normal and 32,500 abnormal.

  2. 2

    Pretrain

    Train a masked autoencoder on the normal X-rays: hide 75% of each image and learn to rebuild it.

  3. 3

    Fuse

    Join image, text, and vitals with cross-attention, and train on twelve CheXpert pathology labels.

Result

The fused model reached a mean AUROC of 0.701 across twelve pathologies, and 0.878 on edema. Whether the clinical context is what helped, I have not measured yet.

Measured on a validation split of 4,922 studies, the same split that chose the checkpoint. There is no separate test set and no image-only baseline yet, so 0.701 is a starting point, not a comparison.

What broke, what’s next

The first long training run collapsed after two of thirty epochs, from mixed-precision bugs and NaN losses. A later audit found subtler problems. The “leak-free” clinical text still carried emergency diagnosis codes that can give the label away, and the text encoder did not match the tokenizer that prepared its input. The next iteration will use the official split with a held-out test set, an image-only baseline, and ablations that measure what each modality adds. It taught me that a multimodal number means little until each modality has earned its place.

Why this belongs in Multisensory Intelligence

The group studies how models learn from many modalities at once, including clinical ones. This is my first attempt at the hard part: fusing an image with the record around it, and proving the record helps. The open question on this page, what each modality contributes, is the question I want to learn to answer properly.

Tools

Python, PyTorch, timm, Hugging Face Transformers, scikit-learn, pandas, DuckDB, h5py, spaCy and scispaCy. Models: a ViT-Base masked autoencoder and a PubMedBERT text encoder. Data: MIMIC-CXR-JPG 2.1.0, MIMIC-IV 3.1, MIMIC-IV-ED 2.2. Compute: one NVIDIA GH200 on Lambda Cloud.

Disclosure

Public. Built on credentialed PhysioNet data, so no patient images or records appear here.
